Prebuilt

Two 3D printers, a resin SLA and a PLA FDM, each learn how far their pins and holes come out from the size they were drawn at. They help each other by sending small summaries of what they believe. Their measurements never leave the site.

1

Every round, one shared plate

The three approaches get the same number of slots on the same build plate, at random positions, so they only differ in what they chose to print. A fixed set of held-out parts is the exam nobody gets to pick.

Hover a part to see what the scanner measured.

    2

    Who predicts the parts better?

    After each round every approach predicts the held-out parts before they are measured. Average error is in microns (µm). The pooled approach sees both printers' raw data, which a real network can't. It is the ceiling.

    3

    Inside a site, agents bid and argue

    Each site runs five agents with different ideas about what to print next. They bid for slots with credibility, and they earn credibility only when their parts make the exam predictions better. Before bids clear, they can challenge each other.

    4

    What crosses the boundary

    One short message per round in each direction: a few shared numbers with their uncertainty, plus how much each agent is trusted. Each site decides how much to listen based on whether those messages have helped its own predictions.

    How much each site listens

    Weight on the other site's messages, from 0 (ignored) to 1 (fully trusted)

    5

    Proof that nothing else leaked

    Every decision and message goes into a hash-chained log at its site. An auditor can replay just these logs, with no access to either site's data, and check what crossed.
