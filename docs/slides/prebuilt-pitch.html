0:00–0:25 · The problem1 / 6

Every shop re-learns its own printer

A 3D printer is only useful once you know how it misses: holes come out small, pins come out fat, edges drift.

Every site learns that from scratch, one test print at a time. Sites can't pool the data, because measurements expose process know-how and customer parts.

Can printers learn from each other without sharing a single measurement?

Hold up a printed plate if you have one. One sentence on why pooling is off the table.
0:25–0:50 · What we built2 / 6

Two sites, one boundary, only beliefs cross

Resin printer · SLA

  • Prints test plates, scans them
  • Agents choose which pieces to print next
  • Every measurement stays here
what crosses
  • Mean and uncertainty of 3 process-agnostic parameters
  • Nothing else: checked against a schema and hash-chained on a receipt

PLA printer · FDM

  • Different machine, different physics
  • Same test plate, same agents
  • Every measurement stays here

Each site fits a physics model of its own printer. Some error terms are shared across machines and even processes (small features shrink, edges drift), and that part is learned together. A third arm pools all raw data as the upper bound.

Point at the middle column: that's the whole interface. Three numbers and their uncertainty.

1:40–2:15 · Results4 / 6

As good as pooling the data, without pooling it

12%

lower model error than learning alone by round 3, in 75 of 100 simulated worlds. Pooling all raw data does no better (12%).

25%

when four sites each have little data: many sites that each know little is where sharing pays most.

0.01

trust left in a lying site after one round. The receipt audit passes in every run: nothing but the schema ever crossed.

Simulation with hidden truth, frozen config, paired per world (hpc/sweeps/relevance). The gain comes from the shared beliefs, not the agent auction. In absolute terms it's 1.6 µm at two sites, small next to scanner noise, which is why the real-printer claim is a committed prediction (next).

Say the honest size once: at two sites it's a couple of microns; the case for this grows with the number of sites.
