How it works3 / 9

Learn locally, share three numbers

1  Print a test plate24 pins and holes. Measure them by micrometer or flatbed scanner.
2  Learn your printerA physics model of your machine, fit locally on your own data.
3  Share three numbersMean and spread of 3 parameters every printer shares. Schema-checked, logged on a receipt.

Never leaves the lab: patient scans, part geometry, measurements, machine settings. A replayable privacy audit checks every crossing: 0 measurements crossed.

0:25–0:50. That's the whole interface: three numbers out, nothing else. Errors every printer shares (small features shrink, edges drift) are learned together; everything else stays local. Results are scored on simulated printers whose true behavior we know.

Results · simulation5 / 9

The win is many sites, not many agents

12%lower error than learning alone, in 75 of 100 worlds. Pooling all raw data: also 12%.
35%with eight data-poor sites, better in 20 of 20 worlds.
0 roundslost to a liar with the consistency check: 180 of 180 lies caught, 0 honest notes dropped.
9 µmsize error from a corrected design, against 132 µm drawn as-is (median, 60 worlds).

Hidden-truth simulation, registered config, paired per world. The consistency check is an added option, off in the registered config. The agent layer adds nothing measurable. At two sites the gain is 1.6 µm, below what a real part can show.

1:10–1:40. Simulation numbers, said plainly. The gain comes from sharing between sites, not from the agents. It grows with the number of data-poor sites. And what a lab actually gets is a corrected design: 9 µm off instead of 132.
