Prebuilt

Printers at different sites learn each other's lessons without ever sharing a measurement.

Agents at each site choose test pieces to characterize a resin printer and a PLA printer from printed plates. Sites exchange beliefs, never data, and every exchange is on a receipt.

The problem02 / 10

Every site re-learns its own printer

0.05–0.15 mm

A resin slip fit. The printer's own bias is often larger than the whole band.

Dozens of plates

Characterizing one machine for one material takes many prints, and it's repeated at every site.

No pooling

Measurements reveal process know-how and customer parts. Sites won't hand over raw data.

Much of what a printer gets wrong is shared across machines and even processes: small features shrink, error grows toward plate edges. That part is learnable together.

The ask: can sites get most of the benefit of pooling without pooling?
The setup03 / 10

Two sites, one boundary

Resin site · SLA

  • Scans and calipers
  • Every measurement
  • Resin parameters: penetration depth, critical energy, bleed, shrink
schema-checked boundary
  • Posterior mean and covariance of 3 shared parameters
  • Agent credibility
  • Unmet-needs questions with a z-score

PLA site · FDM

  • Scans and calipers
  • Every measurement
  • PLA parameters: extrusion offset, polygon shrink, flare

Three arms print side by side on every plate: isolated (own data), networked (own data plus messages), oracle (all raw data pooled, the upper bound).

Shared parameters: size dependence and the plate-position gradient. Anything else is rejected at the boundary and logged.
How the collective decides04 / 10

Agents bid for plate slots

  1. Propose. Five agents rank pieces by what they'd learn: physics, calibration, geometry, empirical residuals, printability.
  2. Auction. Slots split in proportion to credibility, largest remainder. The rule allocates space and never picks content.
  3. Print and measure. Each plate carries 8 held-out pieces at fixed slots, escrowed from every model.
  4. Credit. An agent's credibility moves with how much its pieces improved held-out prediction (leave-one-agent-out, in nats).
  5. Exchange. Sites send belief summaries. Trust in each sender moves the same way, so a bad source fades out.

What does the work, in simulation: the exchange. With messages cut, the same agents with learned credibility do no better than at equal credibility (2.4%, 56 of 100 worlds), and a planted saboteur agent does almost no harm and isn't caught: which pieces print barely matters here. Simulation only.

Be upfront: the auction is how slots get shared fairly, not where the gain comes from. The gain is in step 5.

Robustness · simulation07 / 10

A lying site loses the room in one round

82 µm

Networked error at the honest site in round 1 when the PLA site broadcasts confident, wrong beliefs (isolated: 15 µm).

12.7 µm

Round 3: the bad sender's trust has collapsed and the honest site is back level with isolated (11.8 µm, within noise).

10 seeds. Trust starts at 1.0, so the first round is the price of trusting by default; starting at 0.3 only trims it (39 → 30 µm), but a prototype consistency check that sets aside notes contradicting a site's own data caught 300 of 300 lying notes, dropped 0 of 900 honest ones, and removed the damage. Scaling, 60 worlds each, round 3: more sites alone (2.3 µm) or less data alone (2.7 µm, not significant) help little; four sites that each have little data reach 6.9 µm (27%, 51 of 60), visible through scanner noise. Simulation only.

Many sites that each know little is where sharing pays; that's the honest case for a bigger network. Source: hpc/sweeps/relevance/README.md section 2; replace with the teammate's 2–25-site sweep if it lands.

Next10 / 10

Print anywhere, trust the part

Any shop that can print a flat plate can join and keep its data. It's worth most when many sites each know little.

This weekend's problem is the smallest version: two printers, pins and holes, a few-micron gain. The method matters where tests are expensive, sites can't pool data and results must be auditable: many-machine networks and complex products such as bioprinting.

Released for reuse: the process-agnostic test plate and generator, the flatbed scanner tool, and the hidden-truth multi-site simulator.
