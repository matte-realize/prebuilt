Prebuilt

Printers at different sites learn each other's lessons without ever sharing a measurement.

A collective of scientist agents characterizes a resin printer and a PLA printer from printed test plates. Sites exchange beliefs, never data, and every exchange is on a receipt.

The problem02 / 10

Every site re-learns its own printer

0.05–0.15 mm

A resin slip fit. The printer's own bias is often larger than the whole band.

Dozens of plates

Characterizing one machine for one material takes many prints, and it's repeated at every site.

No pooling

Measurements reveal process know-how and customer parts. Sites won't hand over raw data.

Much of what a printer gets wrong is shared across machines and even processes: small features shrink, error grows toward plate edges. That part is learnable together.

The ask: can sites get most of the benefit of pooling without pooling?
The setup03 / 10

Two sites, one boundary

Resin site · SLA

  • Scans and calipers
  • Every measurement
  • Resin parameters: penetration depth, critical energy, bleed, shrink
schema-checked boundary
  • Posterior mean and covariance of 3 shared parameters
  • Agent credibility
  • Unmet-needs questions with a z-score

PLA site · FDM

  • Scans and calipers
  • Every measurement
  • PLA parameters: extrusion offset, polygon shrink, flare

Three arms print side by side on every plate: isolated (own data), networked (own data plus messages), oracle (all raw data pooled, the upper bound).

Shared parameters: size dependence and the plate-position gradient. Anything else is rejected at the boundary and logged.
How the collective decides04 / 10

Agents bid for plate slots

  1. Propose. Five agents rank pieces by what they'd learn: physics, calibration, geometry, empirical residuals, printability.
  2. Auction. Slots split in proportion to credibility, largest remainder. The rule allocates space and never picks content.
  3. Print and measure. Each plate carries 8 held-out pieces at fixed slots, escrowed from every model.
  4. Credit. An agent's credibility moves with how much its pieces improved held-out prediction (leave-one-agent-out, in nats).
  5. Exchange. Sites send belief summaries. Trust in each sender moves the same way, so a bad source fades out.
This is a sequence, so the numbers mean something. The whole cycle runs in seconds; printing takes hours.
