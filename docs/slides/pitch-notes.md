# 3-minute pitch: script, best points, Q&A

Deck: `docs/slides/prebuilt-pitch.html` (6 slides, rebuild with `python docs/slides/make_pitch.py`). Present mode: double-click a slide, arrows to move, Esc to exit. The demo is slide 3 and never leaves the deck: in Present mode it plays by itself when the slide comes up (about 10 s). **P** replays it, **L** switches the lying site on or off. The caption line under the chart narrates each round, so you can read it as you go. The full 10-slide deck (`prebuilt-deck.html`) is backup for questions.

## Script (~430 words spoken, ~3:10; trim slide 4 if over)

**1 · Problem (0:00–0:25).** "No printed part matches its file. Holes come out small, pins come out fat, by tens to hundreds of microns, and that decides whether two parts fit. That error belongs to your machine today: its resin, its settings, its wear. No datasheet knows it. You learn it by printing, and every shop does that from scratch. They can't pool what they learn, because measurements give away process know-how and customer parts. So we asked: can printers learn from each other without sharing a single measurement?"

**2 · What we built (0:25–0:50).** "Two printer sites, resin and PLA, simulated so we know their true behavior, each with its own model of its own machine. Each prints this test plate, measures every pin and hole, and fits the model. The only thing that crosses between sites is this: the mean and uncertainty of three parameters every printer shares. Nothing else gets through. It's schema-checked and written on a hash chain."

**3 · Demo (0:50–1:40).** It starts by itself. "Each round, both sites print and measure. Only the summaries cross. Blue is a site learning alone, green is pooling all the raw data. Orange is our network: below learning alone, level with pooling, and the data never moved." Press **L**. "Now the PLA site lies: confident, wrong beliefs. Resin gets hurt for one round, its trust in PLA collapses to one percent, and it recovers. A liar costs one round."

**4 · Results (1:40–2:15).** "Across a hundred simulated worlds: twelve percent lower error than learning alone, as good as pooling the data. With eight sites that each know little, thirty-five percent, in every world. And a liar no longer gets its round: a consistency check we added catches its notes on arrival, and never dropped an honest one. What does the work? The sharing between sites, not the agents. Our agent layer adds nothing measurable yet, and we'd rather tell you that than hide it."

**5 · Why simulation (2:15–2:40).** Hold up the plate. "We printed this on a Formlabs Form Studio 1. Its smallest pin should be 1.5 millimeters; with a micrometer it's 24 to 89 microns small, inside Formlabs' own spec and inside the range our simulated printers cover. So the simulator is the right size. But the sharing gain at two sites is under two microns, and two readings of that one pin differed by 65. No part measurement can score that. Only a simulation with known truth can, and we wrote that down before printing."

**6 · Close (2:40–3:00).** "Any shop that can print a flat plate can join, learn its machine faster, and keep its data. The plate, the tools and the simulator are open, and the simulator tells you how many sites it takes before sharing pays. Print anywhere, trust the part."

## The five points to land

1. **Precision is a property of your machine, and you only learn it by printing.** A real Form Studio 1 pin came out 24–89 µm small.
2. **The constraint is real and the design honours it:** no raw data crosses, enforced by a schema and proven by a replayable receipt chain.
3. **In simulation, sharing matches pooling the data** (12.3% vs 12.2% at round 3, 100 worlds) and grows with many data-poor sites (35% at eight, 20 of 20 worlds).
4. **Robust:** a lying site is cut to 1% trust after one round, and with the consistency check (an added protocol option, off in the registered config) its notes are caught on arrival: 180 of 180, 0 of 540 honest notes dropped, no round-1 damage (60 worlds, simulation).
5. **We only claim what can be measured.** Every result is simulated, against printers with known truth. The real printer shows the simulator is the right size; the two-site sharing gain stays a simulation result, by a prediction registered before printing.

## Likely questions, honest answers

- **"Does this beat a single agent?"** Depends which single. Beats one site learning alone: yes, 12–17% at two sites, 35% at eight (simulation). Versus pooling all the raw data in one model: matches it at two sites; at eight it does better, partly because the pooled model is too big to fit well, so say that caveat. Versus one agent per site instead of several: no evidence it's better. With messages cut, learned credibility ties equal votes (2.4%, p=0.27), a saboteur agent barely hurts, and the agents' piece choices don't beat picking pieces at random (1%, 31 of 60 worlds); the network gain survives with random pieces. The win is many sites, not many agents.
- **"Is a couple of microns worth it?"** At two sites, no, and we said so before printing: 1.6 µm, below what you can measure on a part. It grows with the number of data-poor sites (slide 6: 11%, 28%, 35% at 2, 4, 8). Two printers is the smallest possible network; the point is the method, its guarantees, and knowing when it pays. Source: `hpc/sweeps/relevance/README.md`, `hpc/sweeps/coldstart/README.md`.
- **"Did you test it on a real printer?"** One Formlabs Form Studio 1 (Alpha Fast resin), no scanner. We measured pins by micrometer: the 1.5 mm pin read 1.411 and 1.476 mm (−89 and −24 µm). Formlabs' spec is ±0.08 mm on 1–30 mm features. That checks the simulator's scale. We did not fit the model on the real printer (three pieces aren't enough), and we don't claim the sharing gain there.
- **"Then why trust the simulator?"** Its printers miss by the amounts real ones do (vendor spec, our pin readings, published small-hole errors on Formlabs printers), and every result is paired per world against a known truth. Real-site validation is the next step; the plate, hand-measurement sheet and scanner pipeline are built for it.
- **"Formlabs already has Fit Tuning."** That's a manual setting, adjusted by trial prints on one machine. Prebuilt learns each printer's error with an uncertainty from one test plate, and lets machines learn from each other without sharing measurements.
- **"How do you measure without a scanner?"** Micrometer for pins, pin gauges or calipers for holes, by hand. With no tools at all, a pin tried in a ladder of holes measures the fit to one 0.1 mm step.
- **"Couldn't you just share the data anonymized?"** Measurements of a customer's part geometry are the IP. Summaries of three process-agnostic parameters are not, and the receipts prove nothing else left.
- **"Why not start trust lower?"** Starting at 0.3 only trims the first-round damage (39 → 31 µm) and costs a little when everyone is honest. The consistency check does better: each receiver compares an incoming summary with its own local fit and drops it if they disagree beyond what the model allows (chi-square, 0.999). In 60 simulated worlds it caught all 180 lying notes, dropped none of 540 honest ones, and the site being lied to stayed at 11.6 µm in round 1 (64 µm without the check, 11.6 with no notes). It's an added protocol option, off in the frozen/registered config, and this liar is easy to catch (3 prior std off). A subtle liar (0.5–1 std) gets past the check about half the time and costs 11–17% in round 1. Adding adaptive spread (each receiver weights a sender by how much they have agreed so far) caps the round-1 cost at 5% for every liar we tried, against 72–534% with no defence. Also an added option, 60 worlds, `hpc/sweeps/relevance/README.md` section 8.
- **"Isn't your simulation built to confirm itself?"** We ran a negative control: the same 60 worlds, but each printer's "shared" physics drawn independently, so there's nothing to learn from the other site. Sharing then gains nothing: it's 13% worse than learning alone (19 of 60 worlds better), against 10% better (44 of 60) in the normal worlds. So the gain isn't built into the scoring; it comes from physics the printers really share. The flip side, said plainly: if real printers share less than the model assumes, sharing hurts. So we built the fix: adaptive spread, where each receiver learns how far to trust a sender from how much they have agreed. With it, printers that share nothing lose nothing (−0.4%, 30 of 60), and printers that share physics keep the full 10% gain. With the consistency check on too, +1.7% and +10.3%. Added options, off in the registered config, simulation only. Real-site validation comes next.
- **"How many sites do you need?"** In simulation, with sites that start with 4 test pieces and print 2 a round: 11% at two sites (39 of 60 worlds), 28% at four (35 of 40), 35% at eight (20 of 20), after three rounds. Source: `hpc/sweeps/coldstart/README.md`, `hpc/sweeps/relevance/README.md` section 7.
